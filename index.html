// how every course works

The same four moves, every topic.

Each topic is a timed block on a two-day schedule (~8.5 hours per day, lunch included). The clock is the whole point — depth comes from repetition later, not from this weekend.

01 · you already know this

The concept, in plain English, mapped to something in your MERN stack. No math prerequisites, no CS degree assumed.

02 · one thing to internalize

A single snippet or diagram per topic — the template you'll actually reproduce under pressure. Retype it, then rewrite it blind.

03 · spot it in the wild

The trigger phrases: "when the question says X, reach for Y." This is what turns knowledge into a fast answer.

04 · drills with checkboxes

A short problem set per topic — real LeetCode links or whiteboard prompts. Progress saves in your browser, per course.

// and because you'll ask

  • No signup, no tracking, no analytics. Checkbox progress and your theme live in localStorage on your machine. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • One HTML file per course. Save it, email it, open it on a plane — everything but fonts and syntax highlighting works fully offline.
  • Every course tells you what to cut. Only have one day? Each page names the subset worth doing and an honest "safe to skip" list.
  • Verified, not vibes. Complexity claims are checked, every LeetCode link points at a live problem, and capacity numbers survive a back-of-envelope check.
  • Free and MIT-licensed. Fork it, teach from it, send corrections — the whole thing is on GitHub.

Reading is not practicing. Type every snippet, draw every diagram, narrate out loud. Do the two days, then a little every day until the onsite.
